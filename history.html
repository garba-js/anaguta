Introduction

ANAGUTA PEOPLE,
A Brief History

The name 'Anaguta' is derived from two Iguta words: "Ana" meaning "Owner" while "Uta" means "Bow". Therefore, the meaning of Anaguta is "The Owners of the Bow". The language of Anaguta people is called Iguta; which is spoken by all Anaguta with minor variations as a result of dialectical differences. The Anaguta people are subdivided into five (5) clans:

1. Anabor
2. Andoho (Zangam)
3. Anagohom
4. Andigwong, and
5. Andirghiza.

Each of these clans consists of several extended family units referred to as "Ashibi".

The advent of colonialism introduced different religious practices apart from the traditional religions, rites and worships that existed. Colonialism created a range of options in religious choice and practices amongst people. Today, the Anaguta people are adherents of different religious practices, prominent amongst which are: Christianity, African traditional religion, Islam, and Judaism, all bonded by Uguta blood.

A significant proportion of Anaguta land today has been overtaken by development, through physical structures that are a consequence of societal growth. This of course was made possible by Jos being capital of Plateau State. The topography that constituted Anaguta land included high Plateaus, hills and flat land, with beautiful undulations, rock formations, captivating sceneries and fertile land with potentials for agriculture, economic ventures and tourism development.

ORIGIN OF ANAGUTA PEOPLE

Like other preliterate societies and cultures, the origin of the Anaguta people is empirically disjointed and largely mixed up with myths and legends. The constant alteration in the narration of facts and history, being passed from one generation to another is one of the factors responsible for this regrettable state of affairs. There has been attempts by other people to give a somewhat false version of the origin of Anaguta people without success.

A people’s history ought to be narrated by the natives themselves. Any order attempt by different sources will be contrary and certainly misleading. Mythologically, the Anaguta people have one consistent tradition. The elders firmly believe that Anaguta emerged from a cave at a place called "Akotom" located near Shere Hills. This myth is consistent with various theories of the origin of Plateau people, which affirms that the Plateau has been preoccupied for a time beyond living memory. This assertion confirms the Anaguta people as one of the autochthonous ethnic groups on the Plateau.

GEOGRAPHICAL LOCATION OF ANAGUTA PEOPLE

Geographically, Anaguta are located on the hills of Jos Plateau in North Central Nigeria, West Africa.
In specific terms, the Anaguta people are found in Gwong District of the defunct Jos Local Government Area of Plateau State, which had an area of 1,695 sq km and the Anaguta land stretches to about 106 sq km.
Gwong District shares boundary with Toro Local Government Area of Bauchi State and Bassa Local Government in the northern part; in the south by Du District, in the west by the Rukuba and Irigwe people of Bassa LGA, and in the east by Shere District of Jos East Local Government Area of Plateau State. It lies between 9.9°N and 8.8°E. The district stands beautifully on the northern edge of the Plateau named after itself. Its height is about 1,200M above sea level.

LANGUAGE CATEGORIZATION

Linguists and historians have identified "Iguta" as spoken by the Anaguta people as its native language to belong to the Platoid languages in the Benue-Congo group of languages in linguistic classification. (William et al., 1968)

CLIMATE

On the basis of available climate data, the Anaguta people are located on the high plateau region with much more pleasant weather than that of the surrounding plains. The weather, like that of other parts of the defunct Jos Local Government, is cool almost all year round from May to February. As such, the people enjoy a mild climate which has made Jos city a holiday resort both nationally and internationally.

The Anaguta identified seasonal changes (weather) and clearly differentiated the weather into four overlapping cyclic seasons as: Rishi, (Spring)- between March and May, Rukozo (Summer)- between June and August, Uswama, (Autumn or Fall)- between September and November, and Maini, (Winter)- between December and February, respectively.

VEGETATION

Of the five major vegetational zones of the country, the Anaguta people are situated within the savannah region (Guinea Savannah). The vegetation consists mainly of grass except in some nearby villages where thick hedges of cactus have been planted around household farms and compounds. The main farmlands are completely devoid of trees and so are the flat topmost laterite hills. Fringing woodlands and bamboo thickets may still be found along the valley of the more isolated streams.

Generally, the vegetation of the Anaguta people can be described as that of a grassland with dotted trees mostly along valleys, streams, and mountain tops.
